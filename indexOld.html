About

My career started with my hands on the equipment, not behind a desk. I spent six years as a fire control technician, then years more as a quality inspector and team supervisor, before moving into the roles where I now manage the technical data and information systems that large programs depend on to function. Every step added a layer: technical fluency, then process discipline, then people leadership, then systems integration.

That progression is the actual skill. Knowing how to organize complex technical information, train the people who rely on it, and keep a program's data trustworthy under pressure isn't specific to aircraft — it's the backbone of any industry running something complicated.
